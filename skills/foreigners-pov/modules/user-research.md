# User research

**Purpose.** Turn the assumptions that matter into evidence by talking to and watching real people. Evidence first, assumptions second.

**Enters when.** Dissect tagged a high-impact sub-problem as an assumption; the stage is discovery; the riskiest assumption is about what people do or why; or someone asks how to validate an idea, run interviews, or make sense of research notes.

**Stays quiet when.** The question is about whether people will pay or sign up at scale (use `riskiest-assumption.md`), or the evidence already exists.

**Exits when.** Each targeted assumption is marked supported, contradicted, or still open with a next step.

## Steps

1. **Pick 1 to 3 assumptions to test,** from dissect or the ledger. Write each as a claim that could be wrong: "Fixed-fee designers regularly do unbilled extra work because asking for money mid-project feels awkward."
2. **Decide what would change your mind, before talking to anyone.** "Supported if 5 of 8 can point to a recent project where they did unbilled work and name the awkward conversation as the reason. Contradicted if most bill for changes or say it doesn't happen."
3. **Choose the method by the question:**

| Question | Method | How many |
|---|---|---|
| What do people do today, and why? | Interviews about a specific recent time | 5 to 8 per segment |
| What actually happens in context? | Watching them do it, or a short diary | 3 to 5 |
| Can they use our design? | Usability test on a prototype with tasks | 5 |
| How many people have this? | Survey, only after interviews tell you what to ask | 50+ |
| Will they pay or switch? | Not research: a pre-sale or fake door (`riskiest-assumption.md`) | |

4. **Recruit the right people.**
   - Say who, and where to find them (communities, customer lists, support tickets, people who churned).
   - Write 3 to 5 screener questions that check real recent behavior ("When did you last send a fixed-fee quote?"), not self-description.
   - Include people who don't fit the hypothesis: those who never had the problem, solved it another way, or stopped using a solution. They're where you learn you're wrong.
   - Exclude friends, teammates, and anyone you've pitched.
5. **Write the interview guide.** 5 to 7 questions:
   - Anchor in a specific past event: "Tell me about the last project where the client asked for more than you quoted."
   - Follow the story: "What happened next?", "What did you do about it?", "Why?"
   - Ask for artefacts when possible: the quote, the invoice, the email thread.
   - End with "Who else should I talk to about this?"
   - Never pitch, never describe the product idea until the last five minutes, if at all.
6. **Rewrite leading or hypothetical questions before they're used:**

| Don't ask | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| Would you use a tool that...? | How did you handle it the last time? |
| Do you struggle with scope creep? | Tell me about your last project that went over. |
| How much would you pay for...? | What have you already paid or tried to fix this? |
| Is this feature useful? | Show me how you do this today. |

7. **During sessions:** write down exact quotes and what people did, separately from what they said they'd do. Behavior outweighs opinion.
8. **Synthesize.** For each assumption: supported, contradicted, or unclear, with the count (x of n), the 2 or 3 strongest quotes, and surprises. Update dissect tags from assumption to evidence. Add new sub-problems you didn't expect.

## How to report

Keep the reply under about 350 words: the assumption to test and what result would change your mind; who to talk to (including people who don't fit) in 2 or 3 lines; the 4 or 5 best past-behavior questions; one line on synthesis. Offer the full guide, screener, and recruiting message as a file instead of writing them all in the reply.

## What you can do yourself

You can't run interviews. You can: draft the screener, guide, and a ready-to-send recruiting message; turn pasted notes or transcripts into the synthesis; and mine public sources (reviews, forums) as a first pass, labelled as desk research, not interviews. If a user research or research synthesis skill is installed, hand it the brief (router: hand off or suggest).

## Questions (max 3)

- "Which segment first? My guess: <the one where the problem is sharpest>."
- "Can you reach 8 of them this week? My guess: through <community>."

## Writes to the brain

- `learnings.md`: a dated research entry with the plan, then the synthesis.
- `ledger.md` and dissect output: assumptions moved to evidence, or contradicted.
- `state.md`: stage update if the evidence changes the framing.

## Example

Assumption: "People in the city don't know which places are worth a detour."
Recruit: 8 people who ate out in the last week, including 2 who always go to the same places and 2 who use a food guide.
Guide: "Tell me about the last time you went somewhere new to eat. How did you pick it? What almost stopped you?"
Result: 6 of 8 picked from a friend's recommendation in a chat; nobody opened a map first. Supported, with a twist: the discovery moment happens in chats, so that's where the product should meet people.
