# Reframe

**Purpose.** Check whether the problem as stated is the real one before anyone solves it. Sometimes changing what the product is makes the problem disappear.

**Enters when.** A new problem, feature request, or stakeholder ask arrives.

**Exits when.** The person confirms a framing (the original or a reframe). This is checkpoint 1.

## Steps

1. Restate the ask in one line, in the person's words.
2. Find the goal underneath it: ask "so that what?" until you hit a user outcome or a business outcome.
3. Test three reframes, quickly and in your head:
   - **Change the product, not the feature.** Would redefining what the product is make this problem go away? ("keep users after the trip" disappears if it's a daily discovery app that's great when you travel)
   - **Change the category.** Is this the same feature sold to a different budget or buyer? (after-call reporting vs real-time help during the call are different purchases)
   - **Change the constraint.** Is a hidden assumption doing the work? ("it must work offline" was true, but the real loss was never knowing a place existed)
4. Propose a reframe only if it changes what gets built. Otherwise say "The framing holds" and move on in one line.
5. Run "why not just use the giant?" on the framed version. If the answer is weak, say so now.

## Questions (max 3, with a likely answer each)

- "Who is this for, and what are they trying to get done? My guess: <X>."
- "What happens today when they hit this? My guess: they <workaround>."
- "Is <reframe> closer to what you mean, or is the original the real problem?"

## Writes to the brain

- `state.md`: problem as asked, problem as framed, stage, size.
- `decisions.md`: the framing decision if a reframe was accepted or rejected.
- `ledger.md`: the original framing as `killed` if replaced, with why.

## Example

Ask: "How do we keep people using the travel app after their trip ends?"
Read: retention is the symptom. A trip app is only useful a few weeks a year, so any retention feature fights the product's own shape.
Reframe: "It's a daily discovery app for places worth a detour, and it's great when you travel." Local use becomes the core loop; travel becomes the peak.
Giant check: "Why not just Google Maps?" Maps answers what's near, not what's worth going out of your way for. That gap is the product.
